נ Elohai Neshama
אֱ‐לֹ‐הַי, נְ‐שָׁ‐מָה שֶׁ‐נָּ‐תַ‐תָּ בִּי טְ‐הוֹ‐רָה הִיא.
אַ‐תָּה בְ‐רָא‐תָהּ, אַ‐תָּה יְ‐צַרְ‐תָּהּ, אַ‐תָּה נְ‐פַחְ‐תָּהּ בִּי,
וְ‐אַ‐תָּה מְ‐שַׁמְּ‐רָהּ בְּ‐קִרְ‐בִּי.
כָּל זְ‐מַן שֶׁ‐הַנְּ‐שָׁ‐מָה בְ‐קִרְ‐בִּי, מוֹ‐דֶה אֲ‐נִי לְ‐פָ‐נֶי‐ךָ,
יְיָ אֱ‐לֹ‐הַי וֵא‐לֹ‐הֵי אֲ‐בוֹ‐תַי וְ‐אִ‐מּוֹ‐תַי, רִ‐בּוֹן כָּל הַ‐מַּ‐עֲ‐שִׂים, אֲ‐דוֹן כָּל הַ‐נְּ‐שָׁ‐מוֹת.
בָּ‐רוּךְ אַ‐תָּה יְיָ, אֲ‐שֶׁר בְּ‐יָ‐דוֹ נֶ‐פֶשׁ כָּל חַי וְ‐רוּ‐חַ כָּל בְּ‐שַׂר אִישׁ.

The heart of the prayer

Every morning is a return.

Elohai Neshama invites us to meet waking not as habit, but as wonder: breath has returned, the soul is still pure, and life is entrusted to us again.

Traditionally recited as part of the morning blessings, the prayer moves from gratitude for the individual soul toward recognition of the Source of all souls.

Make it yours

A simple three-day practice

Fluency comes from small, attentive repetitions. Use the rhythm below and resist rushing through the words.

Day one

Listen inwardly

Read the transliteration aloud three times. Pause at each comma and notice the repeated sounds.

Day two

Follow the Hebrew

Trace each Hebrew phrase from right to left as you say it, matching sound to word.

Day three

Hold the meaning

Recite one line of Hebrew, then say its meaning in your own words before continuing.
